# Video-guided creative generation: the rules of the agentic workflow (2026-10-16)

Written after re-reading the case PDF ("AI Engineer, Creative Case Study.pdf", 14 pages) and the Unity project
Rollic sent (`~/rollic-case/CBJ`: six levels, the block, grid, gate, timer and fail-canvas scripts). Every rule
cites the page it comes from. These rules govern the third workflow of cbj-creative (`workflows/guided.py`),
which takes a prompt PLUS a video or an image and makes a new creative. The raw text workflow and the edit
workflow are not touched by any of this.

## 0. What the case asks, in its own words

| page | what it says | what it means for this workflow |
|---|---|---|
| p.2 | "turns text, image, and video inputs into controllable 30-60 second Color Block Jam creatives"; "different creative concepts while keeping Color Block Jam gameplay at the center of the output"; the end user "should not need to understand generative AI models, video editing, or complex creative workflows" | the output is always a 30-60 s creative played in the real game; the user types a sentence and drops a file, nothing else |
| p.4 | example request: "Create a creative similar to this reference video, but adapt the concept to Color Block Jam." | "similar" means the concept, not the pixels; the game's own gameplay replaces the reference's |
| p.6 | "A user may provide a creative from another game, an advertisement, or an existing Color Block Jam creative and ask the system to adapt its concept." "Create a Color Block Jam version of this concept." "The goal is not necessarily to reproduce the reference video frame by frame. We want to see how your system can understand the creative structure, idea, pacing, or visual concept of the reference and translate it into a Color Block Jam creative." | the four things a reference can give: structure, idea, pacing, visual concept; the tool reads, understands and translates; it never copies frames; an existing Color Block Jam creative is a legitimate reference |
| p.7 | controlled editing: "If a user likes an existing creative and asks for one specific change, the system should avoid unnecessarily changing everything else." | an edit of an existing creative is a different workflow; a reference-driven NEW creative is not an edit, even when the reference is one of the tool's own |
| p.8 | "Consistency comes first": Unity for gameplay consistency; AI for understanding user intent, creative planning, reference analysis | the video is read by a multimodal model; the gameplay is played and filmed by the real game; nothing in the creative is generated by a video model |
| p.10 | the workflow: provide a prompt and optionally an image or video; generate; review; request a modification; generate the revised version | one prompt box, one drop zone, a result, a feedback loop; the modification step is the edit workflow |
| p.11 | evaluation: multimodal understanding ("meaningfully use text, image, and video inputs"), gameplay consistency, controllability, decision making ("explain your technical choices, trade-offs, limitations") | what the reference gave must be visible and explained; limitations are stated, not hidden |
| p.12 | deliverables: "at least one video-reference-driven example"; a technical explanation with "the main limitations" | the end-to-end set must produce real examples; the limits of the reading go in the write-up |
| p.13 | "The problem intentionally includes areas where current generative AI systems can be unreliable. We want to see how you identify those limitations and design around them." ## 1. The rules

R1. **Input is a prompt plus a file.** A video or an image is never read without words (p.10: prompt first, media
optional). The prompt says what to take from the file. A prompt that says nothing about the file reads it for its
concept (p.6's default request).

R2. **Understand the request before reading the file; the plan narrows what is applied, never what is read.** The
planner's text model writes a reading plan from the prompt and the file's format facts only: focus (one or more of
concept, structure, hook, pacing, captions, ending, look, board), level (none, targeted, full), questions, required
fields, what to ignore, what the prompt fixes itself. The plan wins outright; the word table is the fallback when
there is no plan, never an override (p.8: AI for understanding user intent). Every reference is read in full
(2026-10-17, the owner): the general reading (what it shows and what happens, which game, the concept, the board, the
ending, the hook, the captions, the HUD at the first frame of play, the mechanics and what the game does not have),
with the plan's questions on top as the user's priorities; the ending's last 10 s and the board's still are read
every time. The plan's focus and level decide what goes into the brief first (`applied`; `read_all` is always
true); what the request leaves open is filled from the general reading (R3b).

R3. **The prompt's own values win over the reference.** A stated length, ending, difficulty, replay, tempo, caption
text or look is never replaced by what the video shows (p.2: controllable; p.11: controllability). A stated single
length is the brief's before the play is planned. A range inside the case's 30-60 s ("a new 30–60 second ad", "30 to
60 seconds") restates the case and is no stated length (round 3, 2026-10-17: three ads were made 60 s long with 10-41 s
end cards); a single number is. The request's solve words ("the final solution", "the solve", "a satisfying clear",
"clears the whole board", "finishes the level") make the ending a win over the reference's cliffhanger or time-out when
nothing says near-fail, the clock or a stop before it, and "a moment to breathe", "linger", "let it land", "hold the
solved board" hold the result about 1.5 s longer with no replay of the play (round 3, case 19). Words that imply a win ("the winning move", "after the win",
"once it's solved", "the level completed screen") and an asked replay state the ending too: a replay needs an ending that
has one, so a cliffhanger becomes a win, and an ending the planner set from the request's words is never replaced by the
reference's (round 4, case 30). With the pace taken and the request's own moves, the play keeps the reference's tempo class with no
added hesitation and is shorter, said (a calm stretch made the gaps uneven); a line the request puts on the end card is
shown there once. These round-3 readings are the video
workflow's only; the text and image workflows read the request as before (the owner, 2026-10-17). Relative words for the moves or
the blocks ("fewer moves so each one matters", "a few moves", "fewer blocks") are stated values too: the planner's count
stays, never raised by the play levers, and the shorter play is accepted with its cause said ("the request asks for fewer
moves: 6 kept; the play fills 15 of 21 s"; the owner's trailer-2 creative, 2026-10-17: about 6 planned, 10 played). With no number from the planner the count comes from the
reading (about 70% of the reference's counted moves for "fewer", at least 4; 4-5 for "a few"; about 130% for "more", at
most 14; the reference's tempo class when nothing was counted, said as estimated). On a
cliffhanger the line the request asks for at the end ("End unsolved with a 'your turn' line", "stop ... and ask 'X'") is
the question caption, or the end card's title when the request puts it there, never dropped for the stock question.

R3b. **What the request names is taken first; what it leaves open is filled from the general reading; "only X" takes
just X** (2026-10-17, the owner; round 2 case 14: "Use this ad's hook and its cursor, and show the player stuck once
before solving it" took the hook alone and left the level at any shape, no blocks). Besides the plan's focus, the
board (type, size, blocks, pieces, colours, its look from the measured fill), the ending, the flow and, for a Color
Block Jam reference, the screen are applied from the reading, each only when the request states nothing about it and
says no "only", "just the", "nothing else", "everything else is mine" or "the rest is mine". The look is never filled.
What is filled is marked "filled from the reference (the request left it open)" in the provenance and on the card,
and its rows are info: it was not asked. The request's own ending words ("before solving it") stay its own.

R3c. **Only what is asked is applied, and a revision never undoes the request or the reference** (2026-10-17, round 2
rerun). The pacing is applied only when the request asks about it, and then through the brief's own levers (tempo,
moves, hesitations) to the reference's measured drags a second; a revision restores every value the request states,
keeps what the reference set (the board, the flow's timing, the screen), re-runs the pacing levers on a pacing miss, and
answers "the seconds cannot be seen" with a caption, never with the clock the request asked to hide. A plain solve is a
win; a mistake caption needs a wrong move in the play. The play seconds come first: the tempo and the moves are one
decision (the pacing's rate pinned to the reference's class, or a calm fill), and a revision re-derives the moves from
the take's measure. A viewer's note on what the request does not ask and the reading did not set starts no revision,
and the final version is the best one (fewest misses, then ship), not the last. What the plan says is what was made:
a restored field takes its text back. A stuck moment names one piece (two colours, the mover's colour a single block),
and a wall cell off the reading never cuts the blocks read. A revision of a take over the stated length never
lengthens the play, a revision the viewer alone started changes what the viewer pointed at or makes no version, and
when the play cannot fill the ad the plan says which limit stops it. A taken pace wins over the fill, and a rule that
changes a planned value removes the plan's words for the old one. A speeds-up curve fills with more moves at a normal tempo, calm only when
nothing else fills (round 3, case 16). A concept read or a filled ending takes its wrong moves only from the reading's
timestamped mistake parts, at most one, never from its free list (round 3, case 16: trailer 2 has none), and a revision
keeps that count. A caption promising a chain of exits ("Everything flies!") needs the streak finish, else the tool's
own words (round 3, case 19).

R4. **Read the whole clip in one small copy: Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite first, NVIDIA build as the fallback** (the reader
measured best on the 12 own creatives, 2026-10-16, `READING-ACCURACY-gemini.md`; `CBJ_VIDEO_READER=nvidia,gemini`
reverses the order when Gemini's quota is gone, said in the log and on the card). The whole clip and the ending wait
for their answer (8000 tokens, 300 s, retries); the board's stills are short asks (4000 tokens, no doubling, 150 s, one
try a model, at most 1280 px with NVIDIA first) and the board step's optional asks (the crop, the colour count, the
size re-asks) run only within 300 s of wall clock (round 3: NVIDIA's board step took 1379-2413 s). Any format in (MP4, MOV, WebM, any rotation),
one 360p H.264 copy of the whole clip out; at most 120 s, refused at upload above that with the reason. No cut,
no content measurement before the model has read it; parts are snapped to measured cuts afterwards, only when
the focus needs them (p.6: understand first; p.13: design around unreliability).

R5. **Translate, never copy.** No frame, asset, sound or caption text of the reference enters the creative
(caption text only when the prompt asks for the same words). The look is taken only when the prompt asks for it,
and then only the background and caption colours, never the blocks. A mechanic the game does not have (another
game's bubbles with counters, ice, keys, locks, boosters, bombs, merges, any other obstacle) is never created,
drawn, simulated or used (the owner, 2026-10-17; booster buttons and other on-screen things the build does not have
too): it adds no block, no wall and no shape, and is listed as one line
"<it>: not used: not in Color Block Jam" in the assumptions and on the card, and in not copied; a line of the fixed
table only names the game's own parameter the brief already has ("the ad keeps the game's own near-fail"). The
game's own things under other names (drags, gates, the clock, the result screens, the HUD, grey wall cells) are
native (p.6: not frame by frame; p.8: gameplay consistency).

R6. **Gameplay stays at the centre.** The reference changes only brief fields: the ending, the mistakes, the
finish, the length, the replay, the held result, the end card, the tempo and curve, the hook's function, the
captions' functions, the background and caption colours, and the level's type. When the request says nothing about
the level, the level's type is taken from the reference's board (the owner, 2026-10-17), part by part where the
request states nothing: the shape, the size and the block count clamped to what the generator builds, the colours
(each distinct reference colour to its own game colour, the nearest free one, the mapping shown: "magenta -> red: the
game has no magenta, purple is taken by purple"), the piece sizes; "the game's own level" or "level N" takes nothing.
The blocks' colours are the board's, never the look's: a request for them alone takes the level's colours only, and the
look is the background, the caption colours and the mood. What the reference shows on screen (asked, or with the look):
the camera zoom from the board's share of the frame within the brief's range, the HUD shown or hidden as the reference,
the cursor as seen. The board's top row is never under the HUD (2026-10-17, the owner's "zoom in closer" put the HUD bar
on a 4x9 board's top row): the safe zoom per board is measured on the tool's own recordings (the top at zoom 1 by the
rows beyond the columns, the zoom scaling about the frame's y 1020: 1.12 up to no extra row, 1.1 at one, 1 at two, the HUD
over the top row at zoom 1 already from three); a zoom the request states with a HUD it does not ask for hides the HUD
(the request's words win over a filled value), an asked HUD caps the zoom, and a row measured on the first frame of play
fails when the bar still covers the board and the brief has a lever, feeding the revision (the HUD hidden, or the zoom
lowered); with the clock hidden, a caption carries a near-fail's seconds. No caption may claim what the ending played does not show (a clear, a win or a beaten clock on a cliffhanger, a win on
a time-out): such a line is replaced in the tool's own words, and the guided evaluation checks every caption against
the ending. When the reference is Color Block Jam itself (it says so, or its HUD shows the game's LEVEL n and TIME
mm:ss and no other game is named; a description such as "another puzzle game" is not a name), its HUD's level number
goes to the badge and its clock at the first frame of play to the game's clock (for a win and a cliffhanger; for a
near-fail or a time-out only when the play can bring it down to the ending's seconds; always when the request asks for
it, saying when the ending makes it impossible), each only when the request does not state its own; brief.validate
keeps a starting clock for a win only, so the other endings' clock does not reach the recording. The board is read on
one full-resolution still of the first frame of play (cropped to the board when it fills under a quarter of the frame;
the crop is used only when its box has the board's shape and its reading agrees with the full frame's),
and measured on that crop in code as well as by the reader (round 1, 2026-10-17):
- the symmetry is the code's: the block mask against its left-right and top-bottom mirrors (best axis near the middle,
  intersection over union 0.8), colours ignored; the reader is not asked;
- the colour count is the larger of the read colour names the code's clusters confirm (hue and lightness, three cells
  or more, one cluster per name) and the reader's one count, at most 7; a colour no reader named is never added;
- the size is the median per side of four reads; the fill is the code's share of cells under blocks.
Then: a few inner wall cells give the closest board the generator builds (open, or holes with the fewest walls); a
capped block count shrinks the board to keep the reference's blocks per cell; a mirror-symmetric board takes the
generator's symmetric look and a board with 60% or more of its cells under blocks (measured) its packed look, when the
request names no look; a cliffhanger that stops with 3 or more blocks on the board cuts with 3 left. A reference that
is play from start to end, or shorter than 90% of the ad, keeps the ad play through the brief's own fields (no still
opening, the shortest hold, a calm tempo), and its hook and end card keep their own seconds. A guided revision keeps
what the reference set (the board's type, the ending, the HUD label, the length), as the base version's brief has it
(adapted, never re-derived from the reading), unless the miss fed back is an asked fact about it or, for the ending,
its own row; the board row measures the adapted board, so its miss is fixed by a new take; the length always (a
part-length miss is fixed with the timing fields); and when the play is clearly too short (the case's "gameplay at the
centre" fails, or under 60% of the reference's play), the board's blocks may rise (and its size by a cell a side): the
case's own rule comes before the reference's board. Its exact cells are never copied, how busy it looks is shown only (the difficulty and the
packed look never come from it), and the level is generated and solved by the tool's own rules and played and filmed
by the Unity game, as in the text workflow (p.2, p.6, p.8).

R7. **An existing Color Block Jam creative is a reference when the words say so.** The tool knows the bytes of its
own creatives; with reference words ("a version of this concept", "from this video", "like this", "inspired",
"take only the hook", a prompt that states its own length and ending) it is read as a reference and a NEW
creative is made; with edit words ("same ad but", "keep", "change", "replace") it is an edit; with neither it is
an edit (p.6: an existing creative may be a reference; p.7: an edit changes one thing).

R8. **Say what was taken and what was not.** Every version carries a provenance record (file, focus, level,
seconds analysed, adapted, not copied, mechanics translated, not read when the reading failed) and shows it on the
"Reference to creative" card with the reading plan, the composed prompt and the timeline (p.11: decision making).

R9. **A reading that fails is said, not guessed around; validation is per section.** A "hook" part longer than
max(3 s, 25% of the clip) is play after its first 1.5 s (round 3, case 18: "0-12 s hook" on a 15 s ad); a stretched
comparison never expects a hook over 4 s. HTTP errors, timeouts or an
answer that is not JSON: the creative is planned from the prompt alone with "not read" in the card, the rows and the
assumptions (p.13). A section that fails its check is dropped alone, never the whole reading (2026-10-17, the owner's
trailer: one true gameplay part over the whole clip was rejected twice and everything was lost): parts in the wrong
time unit (the long last part an end card, a result or "other" with under 30% before it) or no usable parts get one
re-ask with the problem quoted, then the parts are dropped ("the parts were not usable: ..." in the analysis, the
card and the assumptions, the part rows info) and the concept, the board, the ending, the hook, the captions, the
HUD and the mechanics are kept and applied. A single gameplay part over the whole clip, or a long last gameplay part,
is valid.

R10. **Only what is read reliably is judged.** The reading's accuracy is measured on the tool's own creatives,
where the truth is stored (ending, captions, hook, moments, part timings). Fields the model reads reliably
(ending, hook, call to action, captions, mechanics) drive rows that can fail; fields it reads approximately
(pacing, part timings within about 1.5 s) are informational unless the prompt asks for them, and then judged
with tolerance and labelled approximate (p.13: identify limitations, design around them).

R11. **One evaluation per workflow, one loop.** The guided evaluation is the text evaluation plus the reference
rows (ending, pacing, hook, purposes in order, part lengths, captions, look, nothing copied, confidence). Misses
feed the revise loop at most twice; a row the loop cannot fix is reported, not hidden (p.10: review, modify).

R6b. **The play fills the ad in every guided creative** (round 4, case 29, 2026-10-17: a 40 s win read for the screen,
the look and the colours played 20.6 s and the engine filled the rest with an 11 s slow replay). With no pace taken, the
play seconds for the brief's length are filled with moves and blocks (the request's own values, size and "fewer/more
moves" win), before the engine's own filling; a replay neither the request nor the reference has stays off while the
play can fill (p.2, p.8: gameplay at the centre).

R12. **30-60 s, always.** A longer reference is brought into the case's range; a shorter one is extended by the
engine's own filling rules (p.2). When the request states no single length, the length comes from the play (round 3,
case 17: a 60 s ad, a 37 s play, a 21 s end card): the reference's own length in the range (28.9 s and 15 s give 30 s)
or the plan's is the most, and when the play levers cannot fill it, the length drops (to 30 s at the least) instead of
the end card growing, said in the play line.

R13. **Test with the case's own material.** The analysis test uses the tool's 12 Unity-recorded creatives (all
four endings) with neutral file names, so the file name cannot leak the ending to the reading plan. The creative
test uses the same creatives plus synthetic footage (title cards, joined clips, re-encoded formats). No other
project's material is used, ever.

R14. **Nothing is generated or edited by a media model.** No image or video model is called in this workflow. The
only model calls are the planner's text model (the reading plan and the brief) and the video reader (Gemini 3.5
Flash-Lite, NVIDIA build's multimodal model as the fallback). The creative is filmed by the game and composited by the tool's deterministic compositor.

### Added 2026-10-19: the video's own board, when asked
When the request asks for THE board of the video ("use the same game design", "this video's board", "exactly as shown";
`videocells.copy_asked`), the board is no longer a board of its type: the first frame of play is read cell by cell as a
picture (the image workflow's reader), verified on the pixels, solved and played as read, with the asked changes made in
code and said (fewer or more blocks, half the blocks, easier, one more or fewer colour, a recolour, a mirror, harder).
"A level like this", "the same kind of level", "a new board of this type" and "create a similar game" keep the type rule
above. The rows: "Video: the board is the video's" and one row per change; the game's door and colour rules on every
version ("Rules: doors and colours", "Rules: colours tell apart"). A colour the game lacks takes the nearest free slot by
RGB AND hue, said in the plan (trailer 2: teal -> blue, magenta -> red, the violet stays purple). When the video is Color Block Jam itself, the copied board is shown in the colours the footage shows (the live game's palette differs from the build's: teal, magenta, violet, lime in trailer 2), each slot's block and door repainted together and the mapping said; a request naming block colours keeps its own, and a revision never repaints from a viewer's remark.

## 2. The test the user asked for

The user's phrasing: "I want a creative as attached video, this style, this flow." Each phrase maps to a focus:

| the user says | focus | what is taken | what stays the prompt's or the game's |
|---|---|---|---|
| "a creative like this video" / "a Color Block Jam version of this concept" | concept (full) | ending, mistakes, finish, length, replay, held result, end card, hook function, caption functions, tempo and curve | the look (the game's own), the level, the play |
| "this style" / "this look" / "these colours" | look | background and caption colours | the blocks' colours, everything else |
| "this flow" / "this structure" / "the same parts in the same order" | structure | length, replay, held result, end card, the parts' proportions | the look, the level, the play |
| "this pacing" / "as fast as this" | pacing | tempo and curve (approximate, labelled) | everything else |
| "this hook" / "this opening" | hook | the hook's function in the tool's own words | everything else |
| "these captions" / "this kind of text" | captions | one caption per function the reference has, own words | everything else |
| "end it like this video" | ending | the ending, the mistakes, a fast finish | everything else |

Order of work: (1) the reading accuracy on the 12 creatives, reported per field; (2) only if the reliable fields
are at or above the previous passes (ending, hook, CTA, captions at 11 of 12 or better, mechanics with no false
claim), the creative set runs; (3) the creative set: the seven rows above each from a different own creative, the
four concept cases (one per ending), the format cases (MOV, WebM), the joined clip with a synthetic opening, the
130 s refusal, the own-creative edit words, the image plus prompt; (4) the findings report and the card check.

## 3. What is already known about the reading (three measured passes on the 12 creatives, 2026-10-16)

| field | reliability | how it is handled |
|---|---|---|
| ending | 11-12 of 12 | drives the ending row; a near-fail is recognised through the near-fail part |
| hook, call to action | 11-12 of 12 | drive the hook and captions rows |
| captions seen | 11 of 12 | drive the captions row; game HUD text kept apart |
| mechanics | 12 of 12, no false claim | the translation table; the game's own screens recognised as native |
| mistakes | 8 of 12 | the count is the larger of the list and the mistake parts; a miss is a loop miss only when the ending is not stated |
| part timings | about half within 1 s, mean error 1.3-1.6 s; snapping to cuts brings moved boundaries to 0.07 s | part lengths judged as shares with a 0.2 tolerance; order judged without gameplay as a stage |
| pacing | tempo word 7 of 12 from counted moves, pace change about half | informational unless asked; then judged with tolerance and labelled approximate |
| confidence | 0.9-1.0 on every reading, wrong ones included | never a gate; shown only |
| board (2026-10-17, v9b: a fresh pass on the 12 creatives, the truth from each `level.json` and brief, the board read on a still) | shape 10 of 12, size within a cell 12 of 12, blocks within two 12 of 12, colours (half each way) 12 of 12; sides with gates 7 of 12; busy against the difficulty 2 of 12 | shape, size, blocks and colours set the level's type and can fail the board row (`audit.BOARD_RELIABLE`); gates and busy are shown only, never judged or applied |
| board, the owner's three references (2026-10-17, refs-v6 to v9: two Color Block Jam trailers and another game's ad, hand truth from frames) | the code's symmetry right on all three in refs-v6, v7 and v9 (block-mask mirror: trailer 3 0.85-0.88, the others at most 0.59; the reader's votes said "not symmetric" every time); the colour count right 2, 3, 3 of 3 (the code never over-counted; it raised trailer 2 from the reader's 3 to 4); the size median 3 of 3 in v8 and v9; inner walls within one 3 of 3 | the symmetry and the colour count from the code measure; the fill (60% measured) decides the packed look; the reader's symmetry votes and "busy" are info only |
| speed | NVIDIA: 19-250 s per reading, median about 60-80 s, occasional server 500s and a reasoning loop; Gemini 3.5 Flash-Lite (low thinking): 4-26 s, no errors | Gemini first, NVIDIA as the fallback; retries with backoff, one re-ask |
